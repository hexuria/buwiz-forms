# Wave 0 diagnostic review lists

Two sweeps across all ten converted forms, now that each has a same-rasterizer
chromium reference. Both produce **candidates for review, never defect counts**.

## Comb-capacity review list

Generated 2026-07-20 by
`packages/form-renderer/visual/tools/comb-capacity-audit.spec.ts` across all
ten converted forms, now that every form has a same-rasterizer chromium
reference to measure against.

**These are candidates, not defects.** The audit counts our declared cells and
the official form's interior dividers inside the same rectangle. A mismatch is
a question for a reviewer against the pinned PDF. Nothing here may be "fixed"
by changing a declared capacity until the official field has been read.

Severity is assigned so review effort goes where it pays:

- **capacity** — a difference of two or more cells at a plausible pitch
  (>= 20 px). This is the class the one confirmed defect belonged to: 2551Q
  Item 12A declared 26 where the official has 16.
- **off-by-one** — a single-cell difference at a plausible pitch. Usually
  ambiguity about whether the field's own border fell inside the measured
  rectangle. Real, but far more likely a measurement boundary than a defect.
- **low-confidence** — measured pitch is 0 or under 20 px, meaning the scan
  locked onto glyph strokes or borders rather than guides. The count is not
  evidence of anything and the rectangle probably does not correspond to the
  official field at all.

## Wave 0 structural review list

Generated 2026-07-20 by
`packages/form-renderer/visual/tools/structural-defects.spec.ts` across all
ten converted forms. Clusters are capped at the twelve largest per page, so
these are the worst defects, not an exhaustive list.

Each cluster is classified by comparing the official and rendered stroke
thickness at the offset where our structure was actually found:

- **weight** — our stroke is thinner than the official one in place. 2551Q's
  largest defect was this: official part boundaries are 1.5pt and we drew 1pt,
  so 1130 px went unmatched while the rule looked correctly positioned.
- **weight + moved** — thinner *and* displaced.
- **displaced** — same thickness, wrong position.
- **missing or extra** — recovers under 90% at any offset, so no single
  translation explains it. Usually structure we do not draw at all.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `0605:1999` | 0 | 10 | 9 | 5 | 4,535 px |
| `0619E:2018` | 0 | 1 | 7 | 4 | 2,321 px |
| `0619F:2018` | 2 | 0 | 4 | 6 | 2,321 px |

**2551Q's largest remaining cluster is 134 px. Every other form is between
1,364 and 5,065 px** — ten to thirty-eight times larger. That gap is what
calibration is worth: 2551Q started this session at 1,130 px on page 1.

Weight defects are common rather than a 2551Q peculiarity, which matters
because the fix that worked there is likely to repeat: official part-boundary
strokes are heavier than ordinary interior rules, and a renderer that uses one
border width for both leaves the top row of every boundary unmatched.

**A correction worth recording.** The first sweep reported zero weight defects
on all nine forms. That was a tool artifact, not a finding: the thickness probe
sampled our render at the *official* position, which returns nothing for any
wholly displaced rule, so every weight defect was misfiled as missing
structure. Probing at the matched offset instead surfaced 81 weight-related
clusters. A diagnostic that reports a clean bill of health deserves the same
scepticism as one that reports a problem.
